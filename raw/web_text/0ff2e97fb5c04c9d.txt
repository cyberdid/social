Borshchahivka clashes: what we know about four injured soldiers found in house
Following clashes in Petropavlivska Borshchahivka, Kyiv Oblast, on 2 November, the Azov Regiment has reported that there were 26 mobilised servicemen in the house being used as a temporary deployment point, four of whom were injured.
Source: a Ukrainska Pravda article, What happened in Petropavlivska Borshchahivka, and what do we know about those who were injured? Azov answers five key questions
Details: Pavlo "Kil" Kurchenko, Commander of Military Unit 3121 of the 1st Corps of the Azov National Guard, to which the temporary deployment point belongs, told Ukrainska Pravda it was not true that "a lot of people had been beaten and one person had died" in the house. These were among the claims made by Valentyna Moloda, a woman posing as a doctor, during a livestream in which she urged people to come to a protest outside the building.
Four mobilised men – Andrii Kuleshov, Ivan Yareshko, Oleksandr Pavliuchenko and Oleksandr Perminov – had sustained injuries and were later taken out of the house and delivered to a National Guard medical facility.
According to Kurchenko, Andrii Kuleshov was in the house "in quarantine to readjust after going AWOL".
Kuleshov had previously served in the National Guard: after being assigned at a military enlistment office, he was sent to the 52nd "Forge" International Assault Regiment within the 1st Corps of the Azov National Guard. He deserted and was put on the wanted list.
Kuleshov was tracked down at his home in Kyiv, detained and taken to the temporary deployment point in Petropavlivska Borshchahivka.
Quote from Kurchenko: "There were certain questions regarding him, as his details appear on the Myrotvorets website. That was because he illegally crossed the border between Ukraine and the Russian puppet republics of the LPR and DPR The Luhansk and Donetsk People's Republics are self-proclaimed Russian-backed quasi-state formations in Ukraine's Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts multiple times between 2015 and 2021. His brother still lives in non-government-controlled territory of Ukraine, in the so-called Donetsk People's Republic."
Details: Kuleshov's mother also permanently resides in the self-proclaimed DPR, but is currently temporarily in Ukrainian-controlled territory. She says she came for medical treatment but plans to return to Russian-occupied territory.
Ivan Yareshko is the mobilised man who, according to another mobilised man, was under the influence of drugs and attacked other people in the house.
According to Kurchenko, Yareshko was mobilised on 30 October and transferred to Petropavlivska Borshchahivka. He was already behaving erratically when he arrived. A rapid drug test showed that narcotic substances were present in his urine.
Quote from Kurchenko: "He [Yareshko – ed.] then attempted to attack the medical worker from our unit who was administering the test. He also behaved aggressively towards other servicemen and was constantly disturbing them, so he was isolated in a separate room. Once there, he began engaging in behaviour consistent with self-harm."
Details: According to Kurchenko, Oleksandr Pavliuchenko injured his back during an air raid when he was going down to a shelter during basic training at a military unit.
Pavliuchenko was diagnosed with a healed fracture of the ninth thoracic vertebra without displacement at the Lutsk City Clinical Hospital and underwent treatment there. Kurchenko said the serviceman frequently violated the hospital's rules of conduct, particularly by drinking alcohol.
Quote from Kurchenko: "Due to severe alcohol intoxication and his inappropriate behaviour – he was urinating in the hospital ward – he was discharged against his will and brought to this temporary deployment point [in Petropavlivska Borshchahivka – ed.]. His hospital discharge summary stated that he needed to remain lying down."
Details: Kurchenko maintains that Pavliuchenko was prescribed medication and placed under the supervision of the unit's medic. No further serious health complications were reported.
The commander does not consider Oleksandr Perminov to be one of the injured servicemen. At one military facility, Perminov had encouraged others to desert. He subsequently went AWOL himself, after which he was detained by military personnel.
Quote from Kurchenko: "To prevent further incidents of personnel being encouraged to go AWOL, he was transferred to another temporary deployment point – namely Petropavlivska Borshchahivka."
Details: The captain added that the unit has official documentation that corroborates the information provided to Ukrainska Pravda regarding all four servicemen. The 1st Azov Corps also assured Ukrainska Pravda that all four men are currently in good health. Internal investigations concerning them have already been launched.
Background:
- On 2 November, the Kyiv Oblast police reported clashes near a house in the village of Petropavlivska Borshchahivka, where servicemen from a National Guard unit were staying. An angry crowd had gathered there following unverified claims that men were being held and tortured in the house.
- National Guard Commander Oleksandr Pivnenko and the leadership of the National Police arrived at the scene.
- Military Ombudswoman Olha Reshetylova has announced that an inspection has been ordered to determine all the circumstances.
- The 1st Corps of the Azov National Guard has commented on the clashes, stating that the deployment point was used to accommodate mobilised men and servicemen who had been detained after going AWOL as they awaited onward assignment to military units and further service.
- The crowd that gathered at the scene attacked a National Guard bus. Kyrylo Budanov, Head of the President's Office, arrived at the scene late in the evening to resolve the situation.
- Journalists from Slidstvo.Info reported that the claim that military personnel were being "illegally" held in Petropavlivska Borshchahivka was spread by a woman claiming to be the owner of a healthcare company called Med-Prestige. The media outlet emphasised that this "private ambulance" has not operated since 2023. Slidstvo.Info also identified the "fake doctor", stating that her name is Valentyna Moloda and she is the owner of a plastic and aesthetic surgery clinic.
- Budanov later said the woman had been detained.
