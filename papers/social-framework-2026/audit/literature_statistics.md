# Primary literature verification: retrieval, coordination, and statistics

Checked 2026-11-10. Scope: nine references requested for the new system paper. Bibliography fragment: [`references_statistics.bib`](../manuscript/references_statistics.bib). Existing case-paper BibTeX was a discovery aid; primary article text, publisher records, author/institutional copies, and DOI depositor metadata were checked again. These works justify method choices and interpretation limits; their evaluations do not validate Social's data or transfer their accuracy to its Ukrainian/Russian incident corpus.

## Metadata exceptions requiring explicit handling

1. **BM25 deposit conflict:** Crossref's current record for `10.1561/1500000019` says volume 4, issues 1–2, pp. 1–174. The publisher PDF's title page and preferred citation explicitly say **volume 3, issue 4, pp. 333–389 (2009)**. The bibliography follows the primary publisher PDF. A DOI match alone would not catch this inconsistency. [Publisher PDF](https://www.nowpublishers.com/article/DownloadEBook/INR-019), [depositor record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1561/1500000019).
2. **Magelinski title variant:** the live publisher title includes initial **A**; the Crossref deposit and old bibliography omit it. Use the publisher wording. The middle initial in Kathleen M. Carley is also present on the publisher page. [Publisher article](https://tsjournal.org/index.php/jots/article/view/30).
3. **Vicente author parsing:** Paula is the given name; Vicente is the sole surname and sole author. `VicentePaula2023` is only the requested citation key. [Publisher article and author information](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11135-023-01615-w).
4. **Broder date precision:** the citation uses the SEQUENCES 1997 conference year. The current Crossref record lacks an issued date; do not pretend the registry supplied a precise publication date. IEEE's live page required JavaScript. The primary paper text was available in the search index of the Princeton-hosted paper; direct PDF opening returned 403. [IEEE identifier](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/666900), [primary paper copy](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr05/cos598E/bib/broder97resemblance.pdf).
5. **Holm identifier:** use the verified stable JSTOR locator. No DOI is asserted in the fragment; a JSTOR numeric identifier should not be silently converted into a claimed DOI. The primary article scan, including title and journal imprint, was located through institutional copies. [Stable record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4615733), [primary article scan](https://sci2s.ugr.es/keel/pdf/specific/articulo/0052_001.pdf).

## Verified works and permitted uses

**Broder1997.** Andrei Z. Broder, *On the Resemblance and Containment of Documents*, Proceedings of Compression and Complexity of SEQUENCES 1997, pp. 21–29, IEEE; DOI `10.1109/SEQUEN.1997.666900`. The paper defines document resemblance/containment using shingle sets and develops compact randomized sketches for estimating set overlap. It supports Social's choice of shingle resemblance and candidate sketching; exact scoring and candidate recall must still be audited locally. It does not make a sketch-based candidate set exhaustive, establish semantic equivalence, or identify a common author/organizer. Primary text identity/title/author checked against the [paper copy](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr05/cos598E/bib/broder97resemblance.pdf); pages/DOI/proceedings checked against the [IEEE-deposited record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/SEQUEN.1997.666900). Conference-year caveat above applies.

**RobertsonZaragoza2009BM25.** Stephen Robertson and Hugo Zaragoza, *The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond*, Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval **3(4):333–389**, 2009; DOI `10.1561/1500000019`. This is a conceptual synthesis of probabilistic ranking assumptions, term weighting, relevance feedback, BM25/BM25F, and parameter tuning. It supports the lexical retrieval baseline used over a captured corpus. Retrieval rank is not a calibrated truth/support probability; a BM25 match does not establish entailment, contradiction, source independence, or search completeness. The publisher PDF title page and preferred-citation text were indexed, while direct fetch returned 403. [Primary publisher PDF](https://www.nowpublishers.com/article/DownloadEBook/INR-019). The deposit conflict is retained above rather than copied into the bibliography.

**Pacheco2021.** Diogo Pacheco, Pik-Mai Hui, Christopher Torres-Lugo, Bao Tran Truong, Alessandro Flammini, and Filippo Menczer, *Uncovering Coordinated Networks on Social Media: Methods and Case Studies*, ICWSM **15:455–466**, 2021; DOI `10.1609/icwsm.v15i1.18075`. Their unsupervised approach projects shared behavioral traces into account networks and demonstrates five Twitter case studies involving identities, images, hashtag sequences, co-retweets, and synchronization. The discussion separates coordination from intent/authenticity and recommends inspecting suspicious clusters. This supports multi-signal candidate analysis; it supplies no universal threshold, Telegram semantic accuracy, or automatic organizer attribution. Metadata checked against the DOI deposit; primary methods/results/discussion read in the [author-hosted paper, pp. 455–465](https://btrantruong.github.io/assets/pdf/uncover.pdf). The publisher landing page was bot-blocked; the [author preprint record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.05658) confirms identity and version history.

**Nizzoli2021.** Leonardo Nizzoli, Serena Tardelli, Marco Avvenuti, Stefano Cresci, and Maurizio Tesconi, *Coordinated Behavior on Social Media in 2019 UK General Election*, ICWSM **15:443–454**, 2021; DOI `10.1609/icwsm.v15i1.18074`. The framework builds and filters user-similarity networks, studies communities, and treats coordination as a spectrum rather than an arbitrary binary label. Its election analysis found coordination and automation indicators largely uncorrelated. That result supports keeping behavioral coordination separate from bot labels; it is bounded by one platform/event and proxy automation measures. Social has not reproduced their full coordination index or established classifier specificity. Primary methods and automation analysis checked in the [CNR institutional paper, especially pp. 446 and 452](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/00ebd390-5f39-42f0-92d4-ed4d8bb49a10/2021_Coordinated%20behavior%20on%20social%20media%20in%202019%20UK%20general%20election.pdf); publication metadata checked against [DOI deposit](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1609/icwsm.v15i1.18074).

**Magelinski2022.** Thomas Magelinski, Lynnette Ng, and Kathleen M. Carley, *A Synchronized Action Framework for Detection of Coordination on Social Media*, Journal of Online Trust and Safety **1(2)**, published 2022-02-28; DOI `10.54501/jots.v1i2.30`. The authors propose multi-view synchronized-action networks and examine the Reopen America Twitter conversation. They identify simple campaigns and a more complex suspicious-user cluster while discussing misuse against marginalized communities. This supports combining behavioral views and careful investigation, not equating every synchronized message with covert manipulation or claiming a calibrated universal detector. Identity, title, authors, publication date, venue, and abstract-level findings verified on the [publisher page](https://tsjournal.org/index.php/jots/article/view/30). No page range is invented for this article.

**Phipson2010.** Belinda Phipson and Gordon K. Smyth, *Permutation P-values Should Never Be Zero: Calculating Exact P-values When Permutations Are Randomly Drawn*, Statistical Applications in Genetics and Molecular Biology **9(1), Article 39**, 2010; DOI `10.2202/1544-6115.1585`. The author-hosted version states publication 2010-10-31 and correction 2011-02-09. It develops discrete permutation inference and explains why naive zero empirical tails are invalid. Section 6 distinguishes with-replacement finite permutation exact calculations from the valid conservative plus-one form; thus Social should say **plus-one Monte Carlo p-value**, not claim it exhaustively enumerated the exact finite null. The source supports nonzero tails and explicit sampling assumptions, not validity of Social's chosen null or its selective hypothesis family. [Primary corrected author copy, sections 5–7](https://gksmyth.github.io/pubs/PermPValuesPreprint.pdf).

**Holm1979.** Sture Holm, *A Simple Sequentially Rejective Multiple Test Procedure*, Scandinavian Journal of Statistics **6(2):65–70**, 1979. This primary mathematical paper derives a step-down multiple-test procedure controlling familywise error across configurations of true hypotheses. It supports adjusting a declared family of valid p-values even with dependent tests. It does not fix invalid component nulls, post hoc event/threshold selection, selective omission, or an incompletely declared research family. In Social, the correction applies to the explicitly declared 16 tests, not the entire historical analyst search. Title, author, year, volume, and pages checked in the [primary scan](https://sci2s.ugr.es/keel/pdf/specific/articulo/0052_001.pdf); issue and stable identity are also visible in the [institutional JSTOR scan cover](https://www.ime.usp.br/~abe/lista/pdf4R8xPVzCnX.pdf). Direct JSTOR text extraction was unavailable.

**Olteanu2019.** Alexandra Olteanu, Carlos Castillo, Fernando Diaz, and Emre Kıcıman, *Social Data: Biases, Methodological Pitfalls, and Ethical Boundaries*, Frontiers in Big Data **2, Article 13**, published 2019-07-11; DOI `10.3389/fdata.2019.00013`. This methodological review organizes problems arising at data origin, collection, processing, analysis, evaluation, and interpretation. It supports reporting selection/missingness, limitations of social traces, and ethical boundaries throughout the pipeline. It supplies a framework, not an empirical completeness rate or an adjustment that makes Social's purposive panel representative. Metadata and article structure/abstract verified on the [primary publisher article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/big-data/articles/10.3389/fdata.2019.00013/full). No local error rate is imported from the review.

**VicentePaula2023.** Paula Vicente, *Sampling Twitter Users for Social Science Research: Evidence from a Systematic Review of the Literature*, Quality & Quantity **57(6):5449–5489**, published online 2023-01-27; DOI `10.1007/s11135-023-01615-w`. The quantitative systematic review examines target populations, frames, sampling plans, collection, sample sizes, and validity in Twitter user research. It distinguishes tweet-level from user-level units and explains how active users can dominate post samples. It supports Social's separation of post-, author-, thread-, and channel-level denominators. It does not establish a Telegram user frame, inclusion probabilities, public-opinion representativeness, or a population confidence interval. Primary metadata, abstract, and sampling-plan discussion verified on the [publisher article, abstract and §§1–2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11135-023-01615-w).

## Acquisition and verification record

Publisher/author/institutional URLs above were searched/opened during this audit. DOI depositor records were read through `GET https://api.crossref.org/works/<DOI>` using the repository Python interpreter and `requests`, with 20-second timeouts. No metadata was inferred from citation counts, search-engine summaries of secondary commentary, or the prior case-paper reference file alone. Search-indexed primary text was used where direct primary download returned 403; these access limits are marked above. The Microsoft-hosted SRC note `src-tn-1997-015.pdf` was inspected and rejected as a Broder1997 substitute: it is the different four-author paper *Syntactic Clustering of the Web*. This prevents a superficially similar primary-document URL from being cited under the wrong title.

The fragment contains nine unique keys and eight DOI fields; Holm intentionally has only its stable record. Publication metadata is kept separate from method support and external validity. Broder and BM25 establish algorithmic foundations; Pacheco/Nizzoli/Magelinski establish related behavioral methods and limits; Phipson/Holm support conditional statistical reporting; Olteanu/Vicente support selection and denominator discipline. None provides independent ground truth for the current case.
